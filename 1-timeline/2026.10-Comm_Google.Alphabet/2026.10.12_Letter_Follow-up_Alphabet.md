Dear Members of the Board and Corporate Secretary,

I am Karel Decherf, a software developer in the EU. I wrote to you in January 2026 about the permanent termination of my YouTube channel **@KarelTest**. I am writing again for three reasons: to correct two statements in that letter, to put the core issue in front of you properly, and to ask for three specific actions. Every statement below is verifiable from my records, and I will provide any source document on request.

**Summary.** YouTube permanently terminated a developer channel with three videos on 15 August 2025, and has never in two years named a single policy provision, fact, video or behaviour behind that decision — not in the termination notice, not in the appeals, not in reply to my direct question, and not to the EU-certified dispute body that examined the case. That body had to close both cases on 16 December 2025 because YouTube did not supply enough information. The channel is still terminated, and the circumvention policy means no new channel is possible.

**Two corrections to my January letter.** I prefer to correct myself rather than leave a claim on file that you might later find unsupported.

1. I no longer claim that my account was compromised, or that the termination was retaliation for my report of a fraudulent advertisement. I checked what I can reach: 2-Step Verification has been enabled since 20 April 2025, no unknown-device sign-in and no security alert appears in my mailbox for 10–16 August 2025, and my data export of 16 August 2025 contains only the three original videos. One narrow question remains open, and it is the reason your Data Protection Officer is copied: Google's interface only displays security activity for the last 28 days, so I cannot verify August 2025 myself.
2. I wrote that YouTube "completely failed to respond" to the dispute body. Two replies do exist, of 30 September and 27 November 2025, and I withdraw that wording. Both were replies to my own emails rather than to the dispute body — the accurate account is in section 3, and it is more serious for YouTube, not less.

**1. No reasons were ever provided (DSA Art. 17).**
The termination notice of 15 August 2025 names one category — "spam, deceptive practices and scams" — and nothing else. In two years there has been no provision, no fact, no video:

- The first appeal was rejected within about 14 hours with "We reviewed your channel carefully, and have confirmed that it violates…" and no named reviewer. The second was rejected automatically because "there has recently been an appeal".
- On 3 September 2025, YouTube Legal Support sent one standard sentence and a link back to the form I had already used twice.
- On 5 September 2025 I asked YouTube directly whether the decision related to "trying to get viewers to install harmful software, or directing them to sites that might compromise their privacy". YouTube has never confirmed that, never denied it, and never named any other provision.
- The replies of 30 September and 27 November 2025 added a second ground — "content where the main purpose is to trick others into leaving YouTube for another site" — which appears nowhere in the original notice and has never been linked to a single video.

Article 17 DSA requires a statement of reasons containing the main facts and the specific provision applied. In this case neither component was ever delivered. That is observable from the record and requires no interpretation.

**2. The appeal process is a closed loop (DSA Art. 20).**
Seven appeal tickets between 16 August and 24 September 2025 each produced the same automated acknowledgement: "Google will review your appeal as soon as possible. Most requests take 2 business days to review." Not one produced an outcome. YouTube's own help page states that creators may file two appeals and that automated replies may be sent once that limit is reached — so the internal procedure was exhausted two days after the sanction, and every further channel (appeal forms, Legal Support, Cloud support, Chrome Web Store support, Developer Relations) pointed me back to the same form. A complaint mechanism that closes itself on day two and then refers users only to itself is not an effective internal remedy.

**3. An EU dispute body could not obtain information from YouTube (DSA Art. 21).**
I filed two cases with Appeals Centre Europe, a dispute settlement body certified under the DSA: #2025-012481-YT and #2025-012487-YT, both on 21 October 2025. On 16 December 2025 ACE closed both, stating in the decision (translated from the Dutch original): *"Unfortunately, the platform did not provide sufficient information to enable the Appeals Centre to reach a conclusion on whether the platform's policy was applied correctly to the reported content. We therefore issue a standard decision in your favour."* The decision record lists only two events: receipt of the dispute and the decision. ACE's decisions are not binding — Article 21(2) DSA says so expressly — and I do not present them as such. What I do present is this: a certified body examined this termination for two months and could not obtain enough information from YouTube to reach any conclusion, while the channel remains terminated.

**Impact, stated plainly.** My nine Chrome extensions remain publishable; the Chrome Web Store does not require a demo video, and I do not claim otherwise. The damage is two-fold: the permanent loss of the demo-video route for future Workspace add-ons with sensitive OAuth scopes, and — because the circumvention policy bars any new channel — the structural unavailability of YouTube as a distribution channel for a film project I am developing. I state this so that any proportionality assessment can rest on facts rather than claims.

**What I ask of you.**

1. A **human decision** that names the specific policy provision applied and the main facts: which video, which behaviour.
2. A **review of the login activity** on my Google account for 10–16 August 2025 — or a written statement that it can no longer be produced. This is the item on which your Data Protection Officer is copied.
3. **Reinstatement of @KarelTest.**

I am writing as a user and developer, not as a litigant. My full dossier — timeline, correspondence, the videos themselves and the analysis — is available on request, in English or Dutch.

Sincerely,

**Karel Decherf**
karel.test.special@gmail.com
